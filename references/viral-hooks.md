# Hooks

Read for retention-style projects when choosing the opening. `hook_variants.py` finds candidate opening lines in the footage; this file is how to judge and dress them.

## Non-negotiables
- **The first frame already has motion and a subject.** No logo, greeting or slow fade.
- **Three layers land together in the first second:** picture (motion or a punch-in), on-screen text (many watch muted) and a sound (the first word, or a hit or riser). virality_score checks all three.
- **The hook must be true to the content, and the payoff it promises must arrive.** A hook the video does not pay off is a fake hook: it raises swipes and hurts distribution.
- Hook types: result first, bold claim, tested-it claim, contrarian take, question, number, mid-action start, visual oddity, stakes.

## Formulas
Each formula gives an example line, then **V** (visual), **T** (on-screen text) and **S** (sound). The `[...]` slots must come from the footage. Never invent a claim the speaker does not make.

### Business and money
1. **Contrarian:** "There's no secret shortcut." V punch-in on the speaker; T the claim in 3-4 words; S low hit.
2. **Result first:** "This one change made us [result]." V the result (chart, product); T the number; S pop.
3. **Mistake:** "Most people lose money because of [x]." V speaker, serious; T "the [x] mistake"; S sub drop.
4. **Myth bust:** "[Common advice] is wrong." V speaker shaking head; T "MYTH"; S glitch.
5. **Cost of waiting:** "Every month you wait costs you [x]." V calendar or clock B-roll; T the cost; S tick or riser.
6. **Insider:** "Here's what [industry] won't tell you." V lean-in punch; T "insider"; S whoosh.
7. **Number list:** "3 things I'd do if I started again." V fingers or a counter; T "3 THINGS"; S pop per number.
8. **Before and after:** "From [before] to [after] in [time]." V split screen; T both states; S swipe.

### Education and how-to
9. **Question:** "Why does [familiar thing] happen?" V the thing; T the question; S riser.
10. **You're doing it wrong:** "You've been [doing x] wrong." V the wrong way; T "WRONG"; S buzzer-like click.
11. **Fast payoff:** "Here's how to [result] in [time]." V the end result first; T the time; S pop.
12. **One-word fix:** "Stop saying [x]. Say [y]." V speaker; T x crossed out; S swipe.
13. **Hidden feature:** "Your [device] can do this." V the feature in action; T "hidden"; S ding.
14. **Rule:** "The [n]-second rule for [x]." V timer; T the rule; S tick.
15. **Comparison:** "[A] vs [B]: which actually works?" V split screen; T "VS"; S hit.
16. **Mid-explanation start:** start on the most surprising sentence of the explanation. V a diagram mid-build; T the key term; S whoosh.

### Story and personal
17. **Mid-action:** start in the middle of the moment ("...and that's when the door opened"). V motion; T a 3-word context; S natural sound.
18. **Stakes:** "If this fails, I lose [x]." V the thing at stake; T the stake; S heartbeat or riser.
19. **Confession:** "I've never told anyone this." V close framing; T none, or one word; S silence then the line.
20. **Twist tease:** "I didn't expect how this ended." V the beginning; T "wait for it" (only with a real twist); S riser.
21. **Time jump:** "One year ago I [state]. Today [state]." V the then/now cut; T the dates; S swipe.
22. **Direct address:** "If you're [identity], listen." V eye contact punch-in; T the identity; S pop.
23. **Unpopular opinion:** "Unpopular opinion: [x]." V speaker; T "unpopular opinion"; S hit.

### Product and ads
24. **Problem-agitate:** "Tired of [problem]?" V the problem; T the problem in 2 words; S negative click.
25. **Demo first:** show the product working in frame 1. V the demo; T the benefit; S product sound.
26. **Social proof:** "[n] people bought this for one reason." V product in hand; T the number; S ding.
27. **Unboxing reveal:** V the box opening; T "finally"; S tear or swipe.
28. **Comparison test:** "I tested [n] [products] so you don't have to." V row of products; T the number; S pop.
29. **Price shock:** "This costs less than [everyday item]." V side-by-side; T the price; S coin or ding.
30. **Use-case POV:** "POV: you finally found [x]." V first-person use; T the POV line; S music drop.

### Fitness and lifestyle
31. **Transformation:** V the after, then the before; T the time span; S hit.
32. **Mistake in form:** "This is why your [exercise] isn't working." V wrong form; T "WHY"; S click.
33. **Challenge:** "I did [x] for [n] days." V day-1 footage; T "DAY 1"; S riser.
34. **Routine:** "My [time] routine that changed [x]." V mid-routine motion; T the time; S ambient.
35. **Quick win:** "Do this before bed." V the action; T the instruction; S soft pop.

### Travel and cinematic
36. **Visual oddity:** open on the strangest or most beautiful frame. V the frame; T the place; S ambience swell.
37. **Hidden place:** "Tourists never find this." V reveal move (drone push, door); T "hidden"; S whoosh.
38. **Cost reveal:** "This trip cost me [x]." V the best view; T the cost; S hit.
39. **Sound-first:** a striking natural sound before the picture lands (J-cut). V the source; T none; S the natural sound.
40. **Speed ramp:** fast motion ramping to slow on the peak. V the ramp; T the place; S whoosh to impact.

### Podcast and interview clips
41. **Strongest line first:** the guest's most quotable sentence. V guest close-up; T the quote's key words; S none, the voice is the sound.
42. **Disagreement:** "I completely disagree." V both speakers (reaction); T "disagree"; S hit.
43. **Surprising number:** the guest says a number that shocks. V guest; T the number large; S ding.
44. **Question to guest:** the host's sharpest question, then cut to the answer's first line. V host then guest; T the question; S pop.
45. **Laugh first:** open on the laugh, then the setup (only when the setup is short). V laughing; T the topic; S the laugh.

### Gaming and reaction
46. **Clutch moment:** start one second before the play. V gameplay zoomed in; T "1 HP"; S game SFX.
47. **Fail:** "This is the worst [x] I've ever made." V the fail frozen; T "worst"; S record scratch-like stop.
48. **Reaction-first:** the facecam reaction, then the cause. V facecam full frame; T "wait..."; S reaction audio.
49. **Rank:** "Ranking every [x] from worst to best." V tier list; T "RANKED"; S pop.

### Explainers and science
50. **Scale trick:** "If [x] were the size of [y]..." V scale comparison; T the comparison; S whoosh.
51. **What if:** "What if [premise]?" V the premise visualised; T the question; S riser.
52. **Counter-intuitive fact:** "[Familiar thing] is actually [surprising]." V the thing; T "actually"; S ding.
53. **Countdown:** "In 60 seconds you'll understand [x]." V a timer; T "60s"; S tick.

## Choosing
1. Run `hook_variants.py --paper P/edl/paper_v1.json --project P`: it scores complete sentences of 1.5-7 s for contrarian, curiosity, stakes, number, question and address words, emphasis peaks and a face on screen, and penalises lines that open on "and/but/so".
2. Pick the candidate that is **true**, self-contained and shortest. If the strongest line is longer than about 7 s, split it: the tease becomes the hook and its answer becomes the first payoff.
3. Dress it with the matching V/T/S from above, or export two or three variants (`--export 2`) for an A/B test.
